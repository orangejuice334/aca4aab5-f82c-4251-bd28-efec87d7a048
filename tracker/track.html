02bHow to measure body fat with the Navy method

The Navy body-fat formula estimates fat percentage from a tape measurement of your waist and neck (and hips, for women). It's the standard the U.S. Navy uses for fitness assessments — accurate to within ±3-4% vs DEXA for most people, and zero equipment beyond a fabric tape.

What you need

  • A soft fabric/cloth tape measure (the kind tailors use). A plastic one is fine. Don't use a metal carpentry tape — it won't follow body curves.
  • A bare midsection. Tape goes on skin, not over clothing.
  • Mirror helpful for keeping the tape level.

Measurements (men)

  1. Neck. Stand straight, look forward. Wrap the tape around the narrowest part of your neck, just below the Adam's apple. Tape should be horizontal — not sloping up at the back. Snug, no slack, no compression. Read to the nearest 0.5 cm.
  2. Waist. Stand straight, breathe out normally (don't suck in). Measure at navel level, horizontal all the way around. Tape snug against skin but not compressing it. Read at the end of a normal exhale. This is usually wider than where your pants sit — measure where your belly button is, not where your belt is.
  3. Height. Cm, no shoes. Already stored in your profile.

Measurements (women) — add one more

  1. Neck, same as above.
  2. Waist — measure at the narrowest point, usually halfway between the bottom rib and the iliac crest (the hip bone). Not at the navel.
  3. Hips. Widest point around the buttocks, feet together, horizontal tape. Snug but not compressed.
  4. Height.

Formula — plug into any Navy body fat calculator online, or compute by hand:

Men: BF% = 495 / (1.0324 − 0.19077·log10(waist − neck) + 0.15456·log10(height)) − 450
Women: BF% = 495 / (1.29579 − 0.35004·log10(waist + hips − neck) + 0.22100·log10(height)) − 450

All inputs in centimeters. log10 is base-10 logarithm.

Tips for repeatable readings

  • Same time of day, ideally first thing in the morning, after using the bathroom, before drinking water. Your waist circumference swings ±2 cm across the day depending on hydration and meals.
  • Don't suck in. Breathe out normally and pause at the bottom of the exhale.
  • Tape level all the way around — front, sides, and back at the same height. A mirror helps.
  • Read twice, take the average. A 1 cm error in waist = ~1% error in body fat estimate.
  • Weekly cadence is enough. Daily readings are noise unless you're cutting fast.

Caveats

  • The Navy formula is calibrated for average body shapes. Very muscular builds (lots of lats, broad shoulders, thick neck) come out lower than DEXA reality — you may read 12% but be ~15%. Skinny-fat builds come out higher.
  • The formula assumes you're not extremely lean or extremely obese. At <6% or >35% body fat the error widens.
  • The number that matters is the trend, not the absolute. As long as you measure the same way each week, the delta is honest even if the absolute is off by 2-3%.
